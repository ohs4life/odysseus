# OHS — GMP (cGMP) Certified Facility

## When to Use
When the user asks any variant of:
- "Is OHS GMP certified?"
- "Where are your products made?"
- "Is your facility FDA inspected?"
- "What quality standards do you follow?"
- "Are your products safe?"
- Customer pushback on quality or a competitor comparison question about manufacturing.

## Procedure
1. State the headline: **Optimal Health Systems is a GMP-Certified Facility.**
2. Clarify the standard: **Current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP) Regulations as outlined by the U.S. Food & Drug Administration (FDA).**
3. Confirm scope: **All products** produced and sold by OHS — made from the highest-quality whole-food ingredients — are produced, packaged, and shipped from the cGMP-compliant facility.
4. For the regulator reference, link to the FDA page: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/pharmaceutical-quality-resources/current-good-manufacturing-practice-cgmp-regulations
5. For deeper quality questions (WADA / anti-doping / professional-athlete use), pivot to `ohs-quality/trushield-certified`.

## Pitfalls
- **Do not call it "FDA approved"** — supplements are not FDA-approved. cGMP means the *facility* and *process* meet FDA manufacturing standards, not that any individual product is FDA-approved. The distinction matters legally.
- **Do not over-claim** about testing frequency, audit schedule, or specific certifications beyond cGMP and TruShield unless you have them in writing. Stick to what's documented.
- **Do not confuse with clinical / pharmacy quality standards** (e.g., USP <797>, ISO 13485) — those don't apply to dietary supplements.

### The verbatim claim (OHS-authored)
> "Optimal Health Systems is a GMP Certified Facility. All products produced and sold by Optimal Health Systems are made from the highest quality Whole Food ingredients and produced, packaged, and shipped from a facility that meets all requirements for the Current Good Manufacturing Practice (CGMP) Regulations as outlined by The U.S. Food & Drug Administration (FDA)."

### Why it matters
cGMP is the **baseline regulatory requirement** for dietary-supplement manufacturers in the U.S. (21 CFR Part 111). It covers personnel, plant and grounds, equipment, sanitation, quality control, production, packaging, holding, and distribution. OHS meets all of it.
